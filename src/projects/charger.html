Case study / Union College Rocket Team Deployable sensor payload

A rocket that drops a sensor payload at apogee and streams five channels of telemetry to a handheld ground station. We built CHARGER and it won.

A G motor flies the rocket to 700-1,200 feet. On the way down the payload releases the nose cone at 75 percent of peak altitude, and the nose cone has to land under 15 feet per second on its own.

With the nose cone gone, a camera on the payload films the ground until it lands.

37 inches, single stage, 2.68 inches across, four trapezoidal balsa fins on a cardboard body tube. Center of gravity at 22 inches, stability margin 1.80 calibers.

Cardboard because the payload transmits from the pad on, and carbon fiber would put a conductive shell around the 2.4 GHz antenna.

The event bans commercial flight computers in the payload, so every ejection charge fires off our own board and barometric sensor. The commercial altimeter is only there so a judge can read peak altitude after recovery.
