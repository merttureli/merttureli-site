Project 06 of 07 . Founder, captain, chief engineer . 2023 to 2026

Founded a team, then won a national event with it

In 2023 Union College had no rocketry team. I started one. In April 2026 it took first place in the Deployable Sensor Payload event at Battle of the Rockets, flying a vehicle named CHARGER, with me as captain and chief engineer. This page is about the part that was harder than the rocket: getting an organisation to the point where it could build one.

Starting from zero

A new engineering club has no budget, no equipment, no safety record, and no reason for anyone to give it any of those things. The first year is not about rockets; it is about building the case that the team will still exist next year and will not hurt anybody. Competitive high-power rocketry has a real barrier that is administrative rather than technical: you cannot fly what you cannot certify, and you cannot certify without compliance, supervision, and a paper trail.

Funding as an engineering deliverable

The budget grew 700% over three years, and none of it was a windfall. Each increase came from a written proposal that had to explain what the money bought, why the team could be trusted with it, and what the college got back. That money funded a high-power airframe and telemetry development, which are exactly the things you cannot borrow. An unfunded design is a drawing.

Safety and compliance

  • Oversaw NAR and Tripoli safety compliance for all high-power launches
  • Chartered the team as an official NAR section, so its standing does not depend on who happens to run it
  • Owned structural design review and the go or no-go call on launch readiness
  • Ran new-member labs that trained 12 people, so capability does not graduate with its founders

CHARGER, on the rail

The competition

Battle of the Rockets is a national intercollegiate competition, judged on design documentation as well as flight performance. We entered the Deployable Sensor Payload event, which requires the vehicle to carry, deploy and recover a payload in flight. I directed the design and manufacture of CHARGER and served as chief engineer through the review process. The result is public record: it is on Union's engineering competition teams page and on the competition's winners list.

What I learned

Schedule risk in a student team is almost never technical. Parts arrive late, people have midterms, and the launch window is set by a range and the weather, none of which care about your plan. The teams that finish front-load the long-lead, irreversible things: certification, ordering, and the safety review. The other lesson is handover. I spent the last year deliberately making myself less necessary, and the charter and the trained cohort are what that looked like. Founding something is only half of it.
