CHARGER

Flies up. Reports back.

A 37 inch rocket that drops a sensor payload at apogee and streams five channels of telemetry to a handheld ground station.

From $2512

Reserve

A G motor carries it to about 1,000 feet. At apogee it drops the payload. On the way down, at 75 percent of peak altitude, the payload releases its nose cone, and a camera films the ground until it lands. From the pad on, five channels of telemetry reach a handheld ground station.

Take it apart.

  1. Airframe

    28 parts, nose cone to motor mount. Two cardboard body tubes, joined by a coupler. The nose cone is a friction fit.

  2. Payload

    Circuit boards, camera, batteries and antenna. The whole upper assembly is the payload.

  3. Motor mount

    A threaded cap holds the motor. Eight screws and the mount slides out the back.

The short version.

  • First place. Deployable Sensor Payload event, Battle of the Rockets 2026.

  • Cardboard on purpose. Carbon fiber would put a conductive shell around the 2.4 GHz antenna.

  • A motor mount you can replace. Eight screws and it slides out the back.

  • Its own board fires every charge. No commercial flight computer in the payload.

  • 13.8 ft/s. Descent, against a 15 ft/s limit.

Look closer.

  • A hollow PETG ogive that friction fits the body tube. On the way down, at 75 percent of peak altitude, the payload releases it to uncover the camera, and it comes down under its own nine inch parachute.

  • The whole upper assembly: circuit boards, camera, batteries and antenna. Its own board and barometric sensor fire every ejection charge.

  • It sits in the forward assembly, under the nose cone. A divider on its mount keeps it apart from the charge that releases the cone.

  • Printed PETG with a threaded cap, so the motor is held mechanically and not by friction. Remove eight screws and it slides out through the back.

  • Four trapezoidal fins seat in grooves in the motor mount, with no adhesive. Root chord 2.76 inches, height 2.36 inches, sweep 2.17 inches.

  • A shock cord mount ties the recovery system to the structure. Descent is 13.8 feet per second under a 48 inch ripstop nylon canopy, against a 15 ft/s limit.
