CHARGER won the Deployable Sensor Payload event at Battle of the Rockets 2026 PAGE. This page launches it again in slow motion. A rigid-body flight model computes the pitch motion with Barrowman's stability method, and a lattice Boltzmann section through the pitch plane shows the air meeting the fins at an angle of attack. A gust or a crosswind tips the nose; the fins, aft of the centre of gravity, push it back.

01 / Method

Method

Two models, two jobs. The flight model flies the rocket. The lattice shows the air and measures the force on the section as a cross-check. Every number carries its source.

  1. 01Flight model

    A planar rigid body with three degrees of freedom (downrange, altitude, pitch), integrated with fourth-order Runge-Kutta at a fixed 1 ms step SIM. Normal-force slopes and centres of pressure for the ogive nose and the four trapezoidal fins follow Barrowman (1967), with the fin-body interference factor 1 + R / (s + R) STD. At large angles of attack the normal force uses sin α and adds body lift, 1.1 (Aplan / Aref) sin² α at the planform centroid, as OpenRocket does STD.

    Pitch damping sums CNα (x − xcg)² over the nose and the fins and adds jet damping from the motor's mass flow (Mandell, Caporaso and Bengen, Topics in Advanced Model Rocketry) STD. Thrust is the AeroTech G77R curve from its published RASP file STD. Drag is one coefficient, 0.6339 FIT, found by bisection so that the still-air apogee equals the page's 1,033 ft PAGE.

  2. 02Flow section

    A two-dimensional lattice Boltzmann model, D2Q9 with BGK collision (Chen and Doolen 1998) and a Smagorinsky subgrid model with C = 0.12 (Hou, Sterling, Chen and Doolen 1996) STD, runs on the graphics card in this tab. The rocket's section through the pitch plane is the solid. Its walls use halfway bounce-back with the moving-wall term, so the body's pitch rate reaches the air (Ladd 1994) STD. The air arrives at the flight model's angle of attack.

    To stay stable with a relaxation time τ0 barely above 0.5 (0.504 at tier 3, set for a lattice Reynolds number of 3,000 at an inflow of 0.10 lattice units) EST the engine adds a regularised collision (Latt and Chopard 2006) and a positivity limiter (Brownlee, Gorban and Levesley 2007) STD, absorbing layers along the edges, and extra viscosity where the local speed passes 0.15 lattice units and for the first 2,000 steps after a reset EST. The outflow edges hold the density at 1 and extrapolate the rest (Guo, Zheng and Shi 2002) STD. Until those first steps are over and the inflow has reached the flight's angle, the panel's lattice readouts show a dash.

    Force and moment on the section come from momentum exchange at the wall links. The panel prints the lattice's centre of pressure and moment coefficient next to Barrowman's. They are a cross-check, not an input: the flight model flies the rocket. A lab toggle (open the page with ?lab) hands the pitching moment to the lattice instead.

  3. 03What the section is not

    It is a 2D slice of a body of revolution. In 2D the air cannot pass around the body, only over and under it, so the section overstates the body's share of the lift, and the fins act as plates of unlimited span. The lattice viscosity is set for a Reynolds number of 3,000 at an inflow uin of 0.10 lattice units EST. Since Re = uin D / ν, the Reynolds number follows the inflow: 900 on the pad, about 2,800 at CHARGER's peak airspeed of 87.5 m/s SIM. The real rocket at 70 m/s is near 3 × 10⁵ (diameter 68 mm CAD, ν = 1.46 × 10⁻⁵ m²/s, the International Standard Atmosphere at sea level and 15 °C STD).

    So the lattice's centre of pressure and moment coefficient are 2D section values. In 2D the long body lifts like a wing along its whole length, with the centre of pressure near its front quarter (thin-airfoil theory, Anderson, Fundamentals of Aerodynamics) STD. At 5° and above, the top and bottom edges of the domain, 0.16 of its width from the axis of a body 0.66 of its width long, also confine the flow and raise the pressure on the windward side: at 5° the section's Cn is 17 to 32 SIM, where an unconfined flat plate of the same length gives 2π sin α L / D = 9.5 STD. Nor does the held flow settle. Held at 5° on the 512 × 160 grid, once developed (after about 2,300 steps), the section's centre of pressure swings between about 9 and 21 in from the nose with a period of about 4,000 steps, several seconds of screen time on a graphics card, always ahead of the CG (21.6 in from the nose at that instant), with Cm +6 to +79 about it, where Barrowman gives 33.1 in and a restoring moment SIM. The panel's lattice Cm is taken about the live CG, which moves forward as the propellant burns, as Barrowman's moment is. The lattice CP shows a dash below 1° of inflow angle, where the section's small offset from the rail buttons (Cn about −0.1 to −0.4 at 0°) would make it meaningless SIM. The lab toggle that lets the lattice moment fly the rocket therefore makes it diverge; it shows what a 2D section would do, not what CHARGER does.

    Inflow speed in lattice units is uin = 0.03 + 0.05 V / (70 m/s), held between 0.03 and 0.10 EST. The 0.03 floor keeps the smoke moving on the pad, so the air there is not still. The inflow angle is held within ±35° EST: on the pad in a crosswind, and through the finless loop, the section shows 35° while the flight model reports more, and the stage says so.

  4. 04Slow motion

    The replay runs at 1/8 of real time until 3 s after burnout, then at real time; the rate buttons in the panel override it EST. The flight model is stepped by screen time multiplied by the rate, so its events keep their real timing while frames take under 0.25 s; a longer frame advances it by 0.25 s times the rate. The lattice runs up to 24 steps per 60 Hz frame at tier 3 EST, fewer on faster displays and fewer when frames run long (the engine sheds steps to stay inside its 48 ms budget), whatever the playback rate, so the flow is a quasi-steady picture at the current angle of attack, not a time-accurate film of the flight.

  5. 05The rocket here and in the case study

    The CAD assembly is 46.49 in long with four fins CAD. The case study describes a 37 in design with three fins and a stability margin of 1.80 cal at a 22 in CG PAGE. Four fins is the final configuration. Shape and dimensions here come from the CAD, the mass from the page (2.2 lb) PAGE, and the CG defaults to the page's 22 in from the nose PAGE.

    The margin shown here is recomputed for the CAD airframe: 4.15 cal at the page CG SIM. The 1.80 cal is the design-review figure for the three-fin design, not a result of this study. The CAD fin root chord (2.76 in) and sweep (2.17 in) match the page CAD; the CAD span is 2.17 in against the page's 2.36 in height PAGE.

  6. 06What the runs show

    With full fins the nose turns into the wind and the angle of attack settles within about a second SIM. With half fins the static margin is −0.35 cal at liftoff, which is unstable SIM; the CG moves forward as the propellant burns, and the margin is +0.14 cal at burnout SIM.

    Without fins the margin is −6.63 cal SIM. The rocket does not tumble end over end: it pitches over and loops. Body lift acts at the planform centroid, 24.42 in from the nose, behind the 22 in CG SIM, so the finless airframe settles at a high angle of attack, 20 to 30°, and flies a loop to an apogee near 340 ft SIM.

  7. 07Checks

    The flight model passes its tests under node (node vx/js/flight/flight.test.mjs): the four-fin CNα and CP against a hand computation, the ogive nose CP at 0.466 of its length, the restoring sign of the moment, the still-air apogee within 1 percent of 1,033 ft after the fit, rail exit against its prediction, energy and NaN guards, determinism, and the lattice toggle (the lattice moment replaces Barrowman's through a 10 Hz low-pass, Barrowman's stays until a lattice moment arrives, and pitch damping is not added twice).

    The lattice's gate is a cylinder 24 cells across in a 512 × 192 grid at a Reynolds number of 100, run for 20,000 steps. An unconfined cylinder at that Reynolds number sheds at a Strouhal number of about 0.164 (Williamson 1996, Annual Review of Fluid Mechanics 28), and blockage raises both its drag and its shedding frequency (Sahin and Owens 2004, Physics of Fluids 16); for 12 percent blockage the gate takes a drag coefficient of 1.35 to 1.55 and a Strouhal number of 0.16 to 0.18 STD. Measured here: a drag coefficient of 1.450 (standard deviation 0.010 over the last 5,000 steps) and a Strouhal number of 0.169 (a lift period of 2,833 steps from 4 zero crossings), after 20,000 steps SIM, both inside the ranges. The run was headless Chromium on software rendering (SwiftShader) at 99 steps per second; it repeats in the lattice lab. Frame and step rates measured under software rendering are not representative of a graphics card.
