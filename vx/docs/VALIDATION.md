## Track L1: analytic checks of the lattice
- Poiseuille channel: two solid wall rows, inflow at left with a flat profile, outflow at right, Re 10 to 50. After the entrance length the profile must be parabolic: compare the centre-line speed and the full profile with the analytic solution for the measured flow rate; error should be second order in grid spacing (run heights 16, 32, 64 cells; report the observed order). This checks the tau to viscosity mapping and the halfway bounce-back.
- Viscosity mapping: measure the effective viscosity from the Poiseuille pressure drop and compare with nu = (tau - 0.5)/3 at three tau values, with the Smagorinsky term off and on (report the added eddy viscosity in a laminar case, which should be near zero).
- Compressibility: repeat one Poiseuille case at u_in 0.03, 0.06, 0.10 and report the Mach error trend.
- Stabilisers' cost: run the same case with the defaults and with setRegularised(false), setMachFloor(0), setStabilisers({sponge:false, positive:false}); report the accuracy difference.
- Grid-size independence of force reduction: a small solid square at three grid sizes, force must scale as expected and the reduction must count every link once (compare with a CPU sum from the readback of the per-cell force texture, if accessible, or with the plate drag scaling).

## Track L2: cylinder series and domain effects
- Re 20 and 40 steady: drag coefficient and recirculation length against the literature (for example Tritton 1959 experiments; Dennis and Chang 1970; Schäfer and Turek 1996 benchmark values for their confined geometry if you reproduce their blockage). Report blockage used.
- Re 100 and 200 unsteady: mean C_D, C_L amplitude and Strouhal (Williamson 1996 experiments, St about 0.164 at Re 100 and about 0.197 at Re 200 unbounded; 2D numerical references list C_D about 1.33 to 1.37 at Re 100). Run at three blockage ratios (domain height 8 D, 12 D, 16 D) and apply or at least discuss a blockage correction; run two grids (D 16 and 32 cells) for convergence.
- Compare the engine's existing validateCylinder result (C_D 1.45, St 0.169 at 12 percent blockage) with your corrected numbers and say whether the method section's statement holds.
- Start-up transient: how many steps until forces are stationary at each grid; compare with the engine's developed flag (sinceReset 2,000 and inflow slew) and recommend a value.

## Track L3: moving wall, lifting surfaces and the CHARGER section
- Rotating cylinder (Magnus) at Re 200, spin ratios 0.5, 1, 2 using setBodyRate about the cylinder centre: lift and drag coefficients against numerical references (Mittal and Kumar 2003 report C_L about 2.5 at spin ratio 1 and about 5 at spin ratio 2, Re 200; cite exact values from the source you use). This validates the Ladd moving-wall term, its sign and magnitude.
- Flat plate at incidence, Re 1000 and 3000, alpha 0 to 20 degrees: lift slope versus thin-airfoil theory 2 pi per radian and against low-Re plate data from the literature; stall behaviour; the pressure difference across the plate. Report how far the regularised, limited scheme deviates from the plain BGK plus Smagorinsky at these Re.
- The CHARGER section from geometry.buildSection at 1024x320 and 512x160, Re 3000 and the page's inflow, alpha 0, 2, 5, 10, 15 degrees with fins at scale 1, 0.5 and 0: measured C_N(alpha), C_m(alpha), lattice CP (cells and inches), fin-only contribution (difference between fins 1 and fins 0), against Barrowman from vx/js/flight/barrowman.js. Quantify the 2D over-read of the fin normal-force slope and the body lift. Try the standard 3D corrections (Helmbold or Diederich finite-aspect-ratio factor for the fins; slender-body theory for the body: a 2D slab section has no equivalent, say so) and report whether a corrected lattice CP lands near Barrowman. This decides what the page may claim about the lattice as a cross-check.
- Pitch damping sign and magnitude: hold alpha 5, apply body rates of both signs, measure the change in C_m; compare its sign with the flight model's C_mq and its magnitude order against the Barrowman-based C_mq (expect 2D to over-read; report the ratio).

## Track F1: flight model internals
- Barrowman components against the original method (Barrowman 1967, and the equations as documented in OpenRocket's technical documentation): nose C_Na 2 and ogive CP 0.466 L; the four-fin C_Na and CP for the measured planform, by hand; interference factor; test a second planform (a known example from the literature or OpenRocket's documentation with published numbers).
- Pitch damping: C_mq formula against Mandell et al. and OpenRocket's documented pitch damping; jet damping term; the linearised natural frequency and damping ratio against a direct simulation (fit the decaying oscillation after a gust).
- Integrator: dt convergence (0.25, 0.5, 1, 2 ms), determinism, energy and momentum sanity, the rail constraint release point and rail exit speed against the closed-form estimate from the thrust curve.
- Trajectory cross-checks: a vertical no-drag flight against the closed form; the full flight against an independent Python integration (scipy solve_ivp with the same forces) within 0.5 percent on apogee and max speed.
- Sensitivities: constant density versus ISA variation to 1,100 ft; motor total impulse plus or minus 3 percent; mass plus or minus 5 percent; cd plus or minus 10 percent. Report apogee and rail exit speed changes.
- Mass properties: the inertia estimate (slender rod plus propellant) against a part-wise estimate from the CAD part volumes with reasonable densities (PETG nose, cardboard tubes, balsa fins, the motor as given): how far off could the pitch frequency be.
