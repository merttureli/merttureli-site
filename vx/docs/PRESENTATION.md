# PRESENTATION.md: from over-engineered to impressive, for non-engineers too

The owner (a mechanical engineer, pilot, photographer, founder; read BRIEF.md) says the engineering impressed him and now the page must also land with non-engineers: a recruiter, a client, a friend. The page: /home/user/merttureli-site/vx/index.html (CHARGER field study; see FIELD.md and reports/field/modules.md). Serve the repo root and open it in Playwright (SwiftShader: slow flow, slow frames; judge the design, not the frame rate; pin ?tier=1 to see the lattice run). Look at the real page at 1440x900 and 390x844, through a launch (?autolaunch=1&speed=1), through the runs, in the pressure view at ?hold=15&tier=1, on the phone with the sheet open and closed, and read the Method, Configuration and Runs sections.

Rules for every proposal: it must keep the honesty rule (nothing fabricated, every number keeps its source tag, no claims the validation cannot back), the design system (Space Grotesk and IBM Plex Mono, monochrome ink ramp with the livery orange as the single accent, grain, one easing, no emoji, no exclamation marks, logbook voice), reduced motion and the no-WebGL fallback, and the phone. Each proposal: what, why it helps a non-engineer or the first ten seconds, where (file and component), effort (S, M, L), risk to honesty or performance, and how to verify it. Rank by impact per effort. Prefer fewer, bigger moves over many small ones; cut as readily as you add.

Lenses:
- first-timer: a smart person with no engineering background sees the page cold. What do they understand in 3 seconds, 10 seconds, 60 seconds. What is noise. What would make them say "oh, that is why rockets have fins". Where do they get lost. Test the page literally: cover the readouts and see if the stage alone tells the story.
- story: a science communicator. The page has a story (pad, rail exit, gust, wobble, fins push back, settle; and without fins it loops). Is that story told in order, with one idea per beat, in plain words, with the engineering readouts stepping back until asked for. Propose a guided sequence (beats, captions in plain language, camera, what the panel shows at each beat) and a plain-language layer for terms (CP, CG, calibre, angle of attack) that stays honest.
- cinematic: a motion and interaction director. The launch should feel like a high-speed camera replay: camera choreography per phase, the moment of ignition, the slow-motion wobble, the fins' pressure flashing in, the settle; the empty stage before launch; the end state; sound is out of scope. What is the one shot that makes someone screenshot it. Where is motion decorative versus informative. Reduced motion must still read.
- brand: a design director who knows his site (BRIEF.md design system). Hierarchy of the display type against 10 px mono, the header strip, the panel density, colour discipline, the chart, the method section as an editorial read, the lab pages as a credible appendix. Does it look like his site at its best or like an instrument dump. Does it make him look like a designer and an engineer at once.
- recruiter: someone deciding in 90 seconds whether to call him. What does the page prove about him, in what order; is there a path to the project page, the resume and the contact; what would a hiring manager at a hardware company screenshot and forward; what reads as showing off versus mastery; what is missing (a one-paragraph plain summary of what was built and why it matters).

Synthesis: merge the five reviews into one plan of at most twelve items, ranked, with ownership by area (page: index.html, main.js, field.css; panel: vx/js/panel, panel.css; scene: vx/js/scene, scene.css; copy: text only), dependencies between items, the verification for each, and an explicit "do not do" list of ideas rejected for honesty, performance or brand reasons. Write it to the scratchpad as PRESENTATION-PLAN.md. The owner's orchestrator reads the plan before anything is built.
