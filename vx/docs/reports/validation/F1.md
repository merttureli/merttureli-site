# F1: flight model internals (validation track)

Date: 2026-10-23. Engine under test: vx/js/flight/flight.js, vx/js/flight/barrowman.js (unchanged by this track),
motor vx/js/data/motor-g77r.js, geometry vx/js/geom/geom.js. Physics was not changed. 70 cases, 63 pass,
7 fail. Every reference names its source; per-case JSON is in reports/validation/F1/<case>.json.

## Verdict

- The implementation is right. Barrowman's slopes, stations and interference factor match the 1966 NARAM-8
  equations by hand to 1e-6. The JAVELIN worked example in Centuri TIR-33 (a second planform) is reproduced to
  0.12 percent, which is slide-rule precision. The RK4 integrator converges: apogee moves 1e-5 between 1 ms and
  0.25 ms. Runs are bitwise deterministic across processes. Energy is conserved to 1e-13 in a vacuum coast. The
  vertical no-drag flight matches the rocket-equation closed form to 1.6e-5. An independent scipy DOP853 integration
  with the same forces agrees to 3e-5 on apogee and 1e-5 on max speed, against the 0.5 percent criterion.
- These checks validate the code, not the method. The page's printed stability numbers rest on inputs and
  simplifications that the measurements expose:
  1. The CG. The PAGE CG of 22 in is the case study's design review figure for the 37 in airframe. A part-wise estimate of the 46.5 in CAD
     airframe puts the CG at 27.1 to 27.2 in at the page mass, which gives a margin of about 2.2 cal against the
     printed 4.15 cal (F02).
  2. The damping ratio. The printed damping ratio (0.094, Mandell's one-degree-of-freedom value) under-reads the
     decay that the simulation itself produces. At constant speed the decay corresponds to zeta 0.142 (B05, B06).
     While the motor burns it is far higher: about 1.0 at rail exit and 0.39 at 98 ft/s, because the thrust turns
     the velocity onto the axis.
  3. Jet damping. It omits the variable-mass I-dot term and is about 5 times too large (B04). The effect on the
     simulated motion is under 2 percent.
  4. The nose CP. It is taken on the 0.232 m nose part, which includes the 32 mm shoulder, instead of the 0.200 m
     exposed ogive (A05). This moves the CP by 0.16 in and the margin by 0.06 cal.
  5. The rail exit speed. It is recorded at the first 1 ms step past the rail top, so it reads 0.34 percent high
     (C12). It also uses the page's rail-button stations. The CAD stations give 43.4 ft/s instead of 46.6 ft/s (C13).
  6. The pitch inertia. The slender-rod estimate is 17 to 26 percent above a part-wise estimate at the same mass and
     CG (F01). The 30 m/s pitch frequency could therefore lie anywhere from 4.75 to 6.46 rad/s against the printed
     5.76 rad/s (F03).

## How the runs were made

- The flight model is CPU-only, so no lattice was involved and no case was time-limited. Node 22 runs the engine at
  about 93,879 RK4 steps per second, so one full flight to apogee (10,053 steps at 1 ms) takes about 0.1 to
  0.3 s of wall clock, and the whole JS side of this track runs in about 12 s. Step rates by dt for the still-air
  flight are 2 ms: 39,540/s, 1 ms: 93,879/s, 0.5 ms: 98,118/s, 0.25 ms: 114,348/s. The scipy
  integration takes 0.7 to 1.5 s per flight with dense output.
- dt, cd = 0, airDensity = 0 and ISA density need hooks the public API does not have. I made test-only copies of
  flight.js with exact, counted text patches: validation/F1/variants.mjs, which changes DT, relaxes the cd, density
  and gravity clamps, and adds an ISA density function and a jet-damping variant. Each copy reproduces the engine's
  history bitwise when the new keys are unused. No VALIDATION HOOK was needed in the engine.
- The reproducible JS runner is in the repo: node vx/js/validation/f1-flight.mjs. Drivers and analysis are in the
  scratchpad under validation/F1/: f1_dyn.mjs, f1_integ.mjs, f1_jet.mjs, f1_cg.mjs, f1_zeta.mjs, and py/*.py. The
  Python scripts run in a scratchpad venv with numpy 2.5.4 and scipy 1.18.1, using python -I.
- Reference documents were fetched over HTTPS into fresh folders and read as data: the Barrowman 1966 report
  (Apogee reprint), Centuri TIR-33 (PSAS archive), the OpenRocket technical documentation (2013), Apogee Peak of
  Flight 193/195/196/197, the OpenRocket 15.03 material database, and the RocketPy equations-of-motion page.

Reading of B:
- The engine computes Mandell's C1 and C2 exactly as Peak of Flight 193 and 195 give them.
- The simulation follows the two-degree-of-freedom linear model to 0.6 to 1 percent at constant speed. In the real
  flight, with a time-varying linear model, it follows to 1 to 3.9 percent RMS.
- The one-degree-of-freedom damping ratio the page prints is a convention of the model-rocketry literature. It is
  not the decay the rocket shows. The normal force and the thrust both rotate the velocity vector toward the body
  axis, which adds damping of alpha.
- OpenRocket's documented pitch damping is a crossflow estimate meant for apogee. At flight conditions it is about
  1.3 percent of Mandell's, so any OpenRocket or RocketPy comparison will show weaker damping by design.

## What could not be done here

- Mandell, Caporaso and Bengen (1973) and Barrowman's 1967 thesis are not freely available, so I used secondary
  sources that reproduce the equations. The C1, C2, omega_n and zeta formulas come from Apogee Peak of Flight 193 and
  195 to 197, which are written after Mandell. The Barrowman equations come from the 1966 NARAM-8 report, which
  carries the same derivation as the thesis. Reading the book would confirm whether Mandell includes the I-dot term.
  Defect 3 does not depend on that, because the variable-mass balance decides it.
