# F2: independent 6-DOF cross-check with RocketPy (validation track)

Date: 2026-10-23. Engine under test: vx/js/flight/flight.js and barrowman.js (unchanged by this track), motor
vx/js/data/motor-g77r.js, fins from vx/js/geom finDefaults. Independent code: RocketPy 1.13.0 (PyPI, the
latest release; Ceotto, Schmitt, Alves, Pezente and Carmo 2021, "RocketPy: Six Degree-of-Freedom Rocket
Trajectory Simulator", Journal of Aerospace Engineering 34(6), doi 10.1061/(ASCE)AS.1943-5525.0001331) in a
scratchpad venv with Python 3.13. Physics was not changed. 79 cases: 52 pass, 11 fail,
16 informational. Per-case JSON is in reports/validation/F2/<case>.json; the figure is
reports/validation/F2/f2_dynamics.png.

Source tags used in the tables: [RP] = RocketPy 1.13.0 as distributed, CHARGER built from the engine inputs
(validation/F2/py/rp_model.py). [RPc] = the same with two RocketPy errors corrected (section "RocketPy errors
found"). Measured values are the engine's, from vx/js/validation/f2-flight.mjs (1 ms RK4) unless the row says
otherwise.

## Verdict

- Both codes use Barrowman, so the static numbers can only validate the implementation, not the method: C_Na
  agrees exactly (7.4729 per rad), the CP to 0.16 mm and the static margin to 0.002 cal (4.145 against 4.143 cal at
  liftoff). The 0.6 mm nose CP difference is the 0.466 L rule against the exact ogive volume formula.
- Still air (constant density, like for like): apogee 314.854 against 314.872 m (-0.006 percent), time to apogee
  8.053 against 8.053 s, maximum speed 87.532 against 87.535 m/s, maximum acceleration 89.66 against 89.64 m/s^2.
  Rail exit speed reads 0.34 percent high (14.201 against 14.153 m/s) only because the engine records it at the
  first 1 ms step past the rail top; its own trajectory interpolated to the crossing gives 14.153 m/s (9e-6).
  With the ISA atmosphere RocketPy gives 316.04 m (1,036.9 ft), which matches the engine's ISA variant from F1 to
  4e-6; the engine's constant 1.225 kg/m^3 costs 0.37 percent of apogee (3.9 ft).
- Crosswind and gust: as distributed, RocketPy turns the rocket 0.42 deg further into a 10 mph crosswind by
  burnout and drifts 4.9 m (9 percent) further upwind. I traced this to two errors in RocketPy's burn-phase
  equations (below), not to the engine. With both corrected, the engine and RocketPy agree on the weathercock
  angle at burnout to 0.011 deg (9.399 against 9.409 deg), on the upwind drift at apogee to 0.8 percent, on the
  apogee to 0.065 percent, and on the gust peak angle of attack to 1.7 percent; pitch histories differ by 0.13 deg
  RMS (largest 0.4 deg at T+0.55 s).
- Pitch dynamics: the oscillation frequency agrees to 0.5 percent in coast and 0.01 percent during the burn; the
  damping ratio agrees to 2.3 percent in coast. During the burn the engine damps 8 percent more (zeta 0.110
  against 0.101). An attribution bridge splits that excess: 46 percent is the engine's jet damping, which omits
  the dI/dt term and is 5.1 times the variable-mass value (confirms F1 B04 against RocketPy's code); the rest is
  the drag axis convention and the engine's body-lift term.
- The engine's printed damping ratio (Mandell's one-degree-of-freedom formula) under-reads the decay that both
  codes simulate: 0.103 against 0.128 at 212 ft/s in coast (confirms F1 B06 independently). The printed natural
  frequency is 4 percent low against the simulated decay.
- RocketPy has no drag estimator, so its "own power-off drag" could not be run as specified. I substituted the
  OpenRocket component build-up (Niskanen 2013) for the CAD airframe and fed it to RocketPy: it gives cd 0.59 at
  Mach 0.2 (band 0.44 to 0.66) and an apogee of 1,061 ft (band 1,027 to 1,159 ft) against the fitted cd 0.634 and
  the page's 1,033 ft. The fitted cd sits inside the band, near its rough end; the apogee is a fit, not a
  prediction.
- 3-DOF against 6-DOF: the RocketPy motion stayed in the pitch plane to 8e-13 deg in every wind and gust run, so a
  6-DOF model adds nothing for a planar wind with no cant and no roll forcing. The remaining 6-DOF terms (CM offset
  and variable-mass coupling) change pitch by 0.02 to 0.03 deg RMS.

## How the runs were made

- RocketPy 1.13.0 was installed with pip into validation/F2/venv (numpy 2.5.4, scipy 1.18.1, matplotlib 3.11.2).
  All Python runs use python -I. RocketPy master on GitHub (fetched 2026-10-23) has the same code in every place
  this report cites.
- Engine side: node vx/js/validation/f2-flight.mjs --out data/js.json records every 1 ms step of five flights
  (still, 10 mph crosswind, the page default 5 m/s gust fired at rail exit, 0.5 rad/s flicks at T+0.8 s and
  T+2.0 s) through the public API, plus every input the RocketPy model needs, read from the engine itself.
  Step rate with per-step recording: 25,191 to 104,353 RK4 steps per second over two
  runs of the five flights (node 22, machine load varies; F1 measured 93,879 per second without recording); one flight takes 0.1 to 0.4 s.
- RocketPy side (validation/F2/py/rp_model.py): the rocket without motor is the engine's dry mass 0.9388 kg
  (liftoff mass minus propellant, so it includes the 63 g case) at the engine's dry CG 0.5251 m with pitch inertia
  m_dry L^2/12 = 0.10910 kg m^2; GenericMotor carries only the propellant, 0.0591 kg as a point mass at 1.0935 m,
  burning as T / (I_total / m_p0), the engine's rule; thrust curve, burn time and total impulse (101.70 N s) are the
  engine's. Mass, CG and inertia then match the engine to 2.5e-7 at every time checked (A01). Ogive nose 0.232 m,
  four trapezoidal fins from finDefaults, constant cd 0.6339 on A_ref = pi r^2 for power on and off, gravity
  9.80665 m/s^2, earth rotation off, LSODA with rtol 1e-9 and a 1 ms maximum step.
- Rail: RocketPy measures travel from the nozzle at the rail base and releases the rocket when the upper button
  passes the top; the engine puts the airframe aft end (1 in aft of the nozzle) at the base, records rail exit at
  the forward button (travel 1.449 m) and releases the constraint at the aft button (1.7792 m). I set RocketPy's
  rail to 1.8046 m so its release equals the engine's rail exit (still-air rows), and to 2.1348 m so its release
  equals the engine's release (crosswind and gust rows, like for like). C15 shows what the rule itself is worth.
- Gust: RocketPy's wind is a function of height only. A Flight subclass publishes the time of every derivative
  call to a shim on env.wind_velocity_x, so RocketPy sees the engine's gust exactly: uniform in space,
  0.5 s one-minus-cosine from T+0.249 s.
- Atmosphere: "const" is RocketPy's custom atmosphere with 101,325 Pa and 288.15 K at every height (1.2250 kg/m^3,
  the engine's value); "isa" is RocketPy's standard_atmosphere (ISO 2533).
- Step rate: RocketPy integrates at about 1,780 solver steps per second (3 to 7 s of wall clock per flight
  at rtol 1e-9 with 1 ms maximum step; 0.1 s at its default rtol 1e-6, which moves apogee by 0.014 percent, B11).
  No case was time-limited; the whole track ran in a few minutes of compute.
- Attribution bridge (validation/F2/py/bridge.py): one planar 3-DOF model, scipy DOP853 at rtol 1e-10, whose force
  terms switch one at a time from the engine's equations to RocketPy's. Its engine end reproduces the engine to
  0.011 deg RMS in pitch (G01, G03) and its RocketPy end reproduces corrected RocketPy to 0.030 deg (G02, G04), so
  each step's change is a measured attribution, not a guess.
- Reference documents fetched into validation/F2/refs-dl and read as data: RocketPy docs sources
  equations_of_motion.rst (v0) and equations_of_motion_v1.rst, RocketPy master rocket.py and flight.py. The
  OpenRocket technical documentation (Niskanen 2013) was read from F1's copy (validation/F1/refs-or).

## RocketPy errors found

These are errors in the reference code, not in the engine. I list them because they decide how the comparison
reads, and because "RocketPy disagrees" would otherwise be misread as an engine defect.

1. Moment reference during the burn (decisive). Flight.u_dot_generalized (rocketpy/simulation/flight.py lines
   2509 to 2515) takes r_CM from Rocket.com_to_cdm_function and r_NOZ from Rocket.nozzle_to_cdm. Its docstring
   defines com_to_cdm as the vector from the CM to the CDM ("com_to_cdm_function plus center_of_mass should be
   equal to center_of_dry_mass_position"), and both quantities come out toward the nose (+0.0265 m and +0.630 m
   at T+0.32 s) although the CM and the nozzle sit aft of the CDM, while the surface CP vectors carry the correct
   sign (nose +0.418 m, fins -0.583 m). The aerodynamic moment is therefore transferred to a point 2 r_cm forward
   of the true CG. Evidence (G05): at T+0.320 s of the crosswind run RocketPy's static moment is -0.50048 N m,
   exactly the bridge moment about x_cg - 2 r_cm (-0.50048 N m) and 18 percent above the moment about the true CG
   (-0.42291 N m). The same rocket built in tail_to_nose coordinates flies identically (py/sign_probe.py), so the
   coordinate choice is not the cause. During the burn RocketPy flies as if the margin were up to 0.99 cal larger
   than the 4.14 cal it prints itself; after burnout r_cm = 0 and the error vanishes.
2. Nozzle term. Rocket.evaluate_nozzle_gyration_tensor (rocket.py line 985) uses S_11 = r_n^2/4 + 0.25 d^2;
   RocketPy's own derivation (docs, Equations of Motion v0, the nozzle-exit integral) gives r_n^2/4 + d^2. With
   0.25 d^2 the variable-mass terms come out anti-damping: -0.0112 N m s at T+0.258 s. With d^2 RocketPy gives
   +0.00349 N m s, the classical result mdot[(x_noz - x_cg)^2 - (x_prop - x_cg)^2] to four digits (G06).
3. equations_of_motion="solid_propulsion" (Flight.u_dot, flight.py line 1931) sets omega1, omega2, omega3 = 0
   after reading them, so the attitude never changes: the crosswind run keeps pitch at exactly 0 (C17). The
   default "standard" equations used everywhere else here do not have this error.

[RPc] corrects 1 and 2 by flipping com_to_cdm_function and nozzle_to_cdm and using d^2 in the nozzle tensor
(rp_model.py, cm_sign_fix and nozzle_fix). The correction moves still-air apogee by 0.03 m (B12).

equation.

## Defects in the engine (model and printed values)

1. Jet damping omits dI/dt (flight.js aeroLoads and barrowman.js jetDamping). The engine uses mdot (x_noz - x_cg)^2,
   5.1 times the variable-mass value mdot[(x_noz - x_cg)^2 - (x_prop - x_cg)^2] (0.01787 against 0.00349 N m s at
   T+0.258 s), which RocketPy reproduces to four digits once its nozzle tensor is corrected (G06). Effect: boost
   zeta +3.7 percent, pitch at burnout in a 10 mph wind 0.04 deg (bridge J1). Severity low. Fix: in jetDamping use
   mdot * ((xNozzle - xcg)^2 - (xProp - xcg)^2) with the propellant station passed in (point-mass propellant, as the
   mass model already assumes); the derivation is the nozzle angular-momentum flux plus dI/dt, whose CG-shift
   terms cancel because m_dry (x_dry - x_cg) + m_p (x_prop - x_cg) = 0. Same finding as F1 B04, now confirmed
   against an independent code.
2. Printed damping ratio (derived.dampingRatio, Mandell one degree of freedom) under-reads the simulated decay:
   0.103 against 0.128 (RocketPy) and 0.131 (engine) at 212 ft/s in coast (E09); the page prints 0.094 at 98 ft/s
   with the liftoff mass. Severity medium for the page's claim, none for the motion. Fix: print the damping of the
   two-degree-of-freedom short period (F1 section B: s^2 + (C2/I + Z) s + C1/I + Z C2/I = 0 with
   Z = (q A C_Na + T)/(m V)), or the fitted decay, and label it.
3. Rail exit speed recorded at the first 1 ms step past the threshold (flight.js detectEvents): +0.34 percent
   (B01, B02). Severity low. Fix: interpolate speed and time linearly in rail travel within the step when the event
   fires. Same as F1 C12.

Not defects, but modeling choices the page should own: drag along the relative wind rather than as an axial
force (2.5 percent of zeta), body lift at large alpha (2 percent of the gust peak alpha), no Mach term in the fin
slope (1 percent at Mach 0.26), release at the aft button rather than the forward button (0.33 deg of weathercock
angle in a 10 mph wind).

## Limits the page should state (measured)

- An independent 6-DOF code (RocketPy 1.13.0, same inputs) reproduces the still-air flight: apogee within 0.006
  percent, maximum speed within 0.004 percent, time to apogee within 0.005 percent.
- In a 10 mph crosswind the weathercock angle at burnout agrees within 0.01 deg (9.40 against 9.41 deg) and the
  upwind drift within 0.8 percent, once two errors in RocketPy's burn-phase equations are corrected; as distributed,
  RocketPy turns 0.42 deg further.
- Pitch frequency agrees within 0.5 percent; damping within 2.3 percent in coast and 8 percent during the burn,
  where the flight model's jet damping is 5.1 times the variable-mass value.
- The printed damping ratio (Mandell, one degree of freedom) is about 20 percent below the decay both codes
  simulate (0.103 against 0.128 at 212 ft/s).
- Rail exit speed reads 0.34 percent high because it is taken at the first 1 ms step past the rail top.
- Constant sea-level density puts apogee 0.37 percent (3.9 ft) below an ISA atmosphere.
- The fitted cd 0.634 is not a prediction: a component drag estimate for the CAD airframe gives cd 0.44 to 0.66 and
  an apogee of 1,027 to 1,159 ft (central 1,061 ft) against the page's 1,033 ft.
- Both codes use Barrowman: their 0.002 cal agreement on the static margin checks the arithmetic, not the method or
  the CG.

## What could not be done here

- RocketPy's own power-off drag estimate: RocketPy 1.13.0 has none (drag is an input). Substituted by the
  OpenRocket component method (section F). Running OpenRocket itself would need a Java runtime and an .ork model of
  the CAD airframe.
- A RocketPy gust as RocketPy users write it is a function of height; matching the engine's time pulse needed the
  time shim described above. A frozen spatial gust (height-mapped) would differ from both.
- Tip-off at rail release (pivoting about the aft button) is in neither code, so its effect could not be measured.
- The RocketPy errors are reported here with evidence; filing them upstream is outside this repository.
