# Track L3: moving wall, lifting surfaces and the CHARGER section

VALIDATION.md track L3. Engine: vx/js/lattice/lattice.js and shaders.js as they stand in the working tree (no physics changed). Reference values carry their source in every row; the full citations are under References. Lattice units unless stated: one cell, one step, rho_ref 1, c_s^2 = 1/3. Force coefficients use the inflow speed u and a reference length (D for cylinders and the CHARGER section, chord c for plates): C = F / (0.5 u^2 L), C_m = M / (0.5 u^2 L^2), 2D per unit span. Moments are in the engine's flight sign (positive raises theta; nose up for a plate).

## Summary

- Moving wall, tangential (Magnus, Re 200, D 20 cells): the lift has the right sign and magnitude up to a surface speed of about 1.5 times the inflow. Spin 0.5 gives C_L 1.293 and spin 1 gives 2.662 against Mittal and Kumar's 1.34 and 2.69 (digitised, about 4 and 1 percent low), spin 1.5 gives 3.874 against 4.08 (-5 percent); at D 32 spin 1 gives 2.546. At Re 100, spin 1, C_L is 2.666 against Kang et al.'s 2.4881 (+7 percent) and St 0.1645 against 0.1655. Drag is 5 to 14 percent high, the staircase-wall excess track L2 measured on the plain cylinder.
- At higher spin it fails: spin 2 gives C_L 4.969 against about 5.6 (-11 percent) and spin 3 gives 7.166 against 10.366 (-31 percent), and both stay unsteady where the reference flow is steady (C_L swings 0.97 and 1.26). With the Smagorinsky term off, spin 2 settles on a wrong flow with negative mean lift.
- Moving wall, normal to the surface (the case the page's pitch coupling uses): a plate at rest with a uniform wall velocity v lifts 2.0 to 2.5 times as much as the same plate at the Galilean-equivalent incidence -atan(v/u), on two chords and two thicknesses. The pitch-rate response of a plate is 2.4 times (pivot at the quarter chord) and 2.6 times (mid-chord) the thin-airfoil value relative to the plate's own lift slope.
- Flat plate at Re 1000 (chord 48 cells, 2 cells thick): lift slope 5.136 per rad (0 to 4 deg), 82 percent of 2 pi and 5 percent under the 2D Fluent value of Ashraf et al. (2026); C_D at 0, 10 and 15 deg within 3 percent of theirs; the pressure difference across the plate matches thin-airfoil theory within 1 percent at mid-chord at 4 deg; the centre of pressure sits at 0.27 c and moves aft to 0.40 c by 25 deg. Above 15 deg lift and drag run 18 to 26 percent over that reference.
- At Re 3000 the lift slope is 4.586 per rad at chord 48, 73 percent of 2 pi and below the Re 1000 value; it moves with the grid (3.606 at chord 32, 4.586 at chord 48, 4.925 at chord 64), does not rise with the Smagorinsky term off (4.462), and is 5.134 with the BGK + Smagorinsky collision the brief asks about (absorbing edges kept): the regularised, limited scheme costs 11 percent of the lift slope at Re 3000 and 1.5 percent at Re 1000 (regularised collision alone 4.586, positivity limiter alone 5.134). Without the absorbing edges the plain scheme fills with acoustic noise (max abs(rho - 1) 0.17 against 0.017) and its forces are not usable.
- CHARGER section (geom.buildSection, page grid 512 x 160, Re 3000, inflow 0.10): the lattice centre of pressure sits at 15.6 in from the nose at 5 deg against Barrowman's 31.3 in, ahead of the 22 in CG at every angle and grid, so the 2D section is statically unstable where the rocket is stable by 4.1 calibres. Used as 3D coefficients the way the page uses them, the section's C_N is 21 times Barrowman's at 5 deg, its fin increment 12 times, its finless body 35 times.
- Most of the page-domain C_N is confinement: the same section in a domain 3.2 times taller carries 0.58 of it at 5 deg, close to the thin-airfoil value of a plate the length of the rocket; the centre of pressure does not move (15.6 in). The links on the fins themselves carry 1 to 10 percent of the fin increment; the rest is extra circulation on the body.
- The standard 3D corrections do not rescue the section: replacing the 2D body by slender-body theory and passing the section's fin increment through the Diederich finite-wing formula puts the CP at 15.6 in (Barrowman 33.1 in), because the increment acts at 18.7 in, not on the fins. Taking only a section lift slope from the lattice (its flat plate at the fin chord Reynolds number, 4.71 per rad) and everything else from Barrowman gives 32.1 in, which checks the planform method, not the section.
- Pitch damping (alpha 5 deg, body rates of both signs): the sign is right in every run (a nose-up rate lowers C_m). The 2D magnitude, -dC_m/d(qD/V) = 695 with fins and 1423 without, is 1.6 and 16 times Barrowman's C_mq (445 and 88), against a 2D thin-airfoil value of 24 for this length and CG; with the normal-wall over-read above, the lattice's damping is not a usable number.
- 42 of 86 cases pass their stated tolerance. Every long run used a single-thread CPU transcription of the engine step (29 to 68 MCUPS per run here), checked against the engine on SwiftShader: identical to 1e-3 until the flow amplifies round-off (step 2,300 to 2,750 in the unsteady cases, never in 12,000 steps of a spinning cylinder), and statistically identical after that.

## Method

**What ran.** SwiftShader ran the engine at 13 to 153 steps per second on the L3 grids (1.9 to 9.4 MCUPS, depending on the grid and on the other tracks sharing the 4 cores), so one 135,000-step tier-3 sweep would take over an hour per fin scale. As track L2 did, the step pass of vx/js/lattice/shaders.js (STEP_FRAG: edges, wall links with the moving-wall term and the Galilean-corrected momentum exchange, BGK with Smagorinsky, regularisation, speed floor, absorbing edges, positivity limiter) and the host logic of lattice.js (reset to uniform inflow with the body rate already at its target, start-up viscosity 0.75 easing to tau0 over 2,000 steps, inflow slew 1e-4 per step per component, body-rate slew 2e-5 / max(1, r_max) per step, tau0 = 0.5 + 3 u_ref D / Re) run on the CPU in float32: scratchpad validation/L3/l3lbm.c, which is L2's verified l2lbm3.c kernel unchanged plus a schedule of phases (alpha, body rate), per-region force sums (body, fin cells) and per-phase mean fields. It reproduces l2lbm3 to 1.3e-6 on L2's case and the engine as section 1 shows. Each run is one process in its own session; a queue kept 3 to 4 running.

**Runners.** GPU: vx/lab/validation/l3.html with vx/js/validation/l3-lift.js (public API only: setSection, setViscosity, setSmagorinsky, setRegularised, setMachFloor, setStabilisers, setInflow, setBodyRate, reset, step, sampleForce, check), driven by scratchpad validation/L3/gpu.cjs (Playwright, SwiftShader, clarity.ms, bing.com, workers.dev and formspree.io blocked, no POST). CPU: cpu.cjs, queue.cjs and cpu_diag.sh (the diagnostic binary l3lbm_diag.c, see section 3). Masks: scripts/gen_masks.mjs and gen_masks_big.mjs (geom.buildSection on the baked CAD copy, the page's defaults: view through, rail buttons, PAGE CG 22 in). Analyses: scripts/*.py; collect.py writes summary.json, this report comes from write_report.py.

**Averaging.** Forces are means per 50-step chunk of the per-step link sums. Magnus: the last 40 percent of the run, over whole lift cycles when it sheds. Plate and CHARGER sweeps: each angle is held a fixed number of chord passes (plate 16, means over the last 10; CHARGER 4 passes of the rocket length, means over the last 2), with the inflow slewing to the next angle. CHARGER centre of pressure: x_cg - C_m D / C_N in cells, then (x - x_nose) cell_m / 0.0254 in from the nose.

**References.** Mittal and Kumar (2003) could not be downloaded here, so their Re 200 values come from two published copies: the tabulated values at spin 3 to 5 in Padrino and Joseph (2006, Table 2) and their data points as plotted by Kalinin and Mazo (2013, Fig. 14), digitised here (method and accuracy under References). The plate references are 2D simulations (Ashraf et al. 2026; Yang et al. as they quote) and thin-airfoil theory. No 2D plate data at Re 3000 was found in what could be retrieved.

The low-spin agreement validates the sign and the magnitude of Ladd's term for a wall that moves along itself, at the surface speeds the page uses (its body-rate mapping caps the wall speed at 0.05 lattice units at the body ends). Above a surface speed of about 1.5 times the inflow, a staircase circle with halfway bounce-back loses circulation and generates an unsteady flow at 20 cells per diameter (spin 2 recovers to 3 percent at 32 cells); the domain height (20 D against 40 D) changes nothing, and switching the Smagorinsky term off makes spin 1 10 percent worse and spin 2 wrong; L2's proposed interpolated bounce-back (Bouzidi et al. 2001) with the wall velocity at the true wall point is the standard cure and would need this series re-run.

## 3. Moving wall with a velocity normal to the surface: plate pitch rate and plunge

The page drives the section's pitch rate through setBodyRate on a static mask, so along the long body the wall velocity is mostly normal to the surface. Two tests isolate that use of the term, each against a reference that does not depend on the domain or on the viscous lift loss, because the reference is formed from the same run's own static response.

**Pitch rate.** Plate chord 48, 2 cells thick, domain 576 x 576 (12 chords tall), Re 1000. Phases at alpha 0 with Q = q c / V = 0, +-0.05 and +-0.1, and at alpha +-2 deg without rate, so dc_l/dalpha and dc_l/dQ come from one run. Thin-airfoil theory (equivalent camber) gives dc_l/dQ = 2 pi (3/4 - h) and the ratio (dc_l/dQ) / (dc_l/dalpha) = 3/4 - h for a pivot at h chords.

A static wall that is given a normal velocity injects mass on the face that moves into the fluid and removes it on the face that moves away (Ladd's term changes the reflected population by 6 w_k rho e_k.u_w). The real body displaces fluid around itself instead. The measured result is a lift 2.0 to 2.5 times the physical one, from the faces themselves (the leading and trailing edge faces add about 15 percent), and it does not shrink with a finer chord or a thicker plate. Everything the page derives from setBodyRate in flight, the damping of the lattice moment and the flick response, inherits this factor on top of the 2D-section effects of section 5.

## 8. Defects

1. **vx/js/lattice/shaders.js stepFrag, wall-link branch (vec2 uw = uOmega * vec2(-d.y, d.x); f_k = s_opp + 6 W_k rhoW (e_k . uw)), driven by vx/js/lattice/lattice.js setBodyRate and the page's pitchRateToLattice mapping (vx/js/main.js latticeFrame)** (major). The pitch rate is applied as a moving-wall velocity on a static mask. Where that velocity is normal to the surface (along most of a long body pitching about its CG) the term acts as transpiration: it creates mass on the face moving into the fluid and removes it on the other, instead of displacing the fluid round the body, and the resulting force is about 2.3 times the physical one. Tangential wall motion (a spinning cylinder) is not affected. Evidence: Plate at rest with a uniform wall velocity v = 0.04 u (diagnostic option plunge) against the same plate at the Galilean-equivalent incidence: lift ratio 2.31, 1.98, 2.21, 2.49 for chord 48 / 48 without edge faces / 64 / 48 with 4 cells thickness (Re 1000, 12 chords tall; target 1). Plate pitch rate on the transcription: (dc_l/dQ)/(dc_l/dalpha) 1.189 at a quarter-chord pivot and 0.638 at mid-chord against 0.5 and 0.25 from thin-airfoil theory; dc_m,c/4/dQ -0.711 against -0.33. The engine itself on SwiftShader gives the same ratio (1.184 against 1.184 on the transcription, chord 32). Reports: reports/validation/L3/pqd-qc.json, pqd-mid.json, pl-still-plunge*.json. Proposed fix: Apply a body rate in the body frame instead: walls at rest (u_w = 0 in the wall links) and the edge cells fed with the relative wind u_in - Omega x (r - r_cg), plus the Coriolis acceleration -2 Omega x u as a forcing term in the collision (Guo, Zheng and Shi 2002 forcing; the centrifugal term is negligible at these rates). Re-run the plate pitch test (target ratio 3/4 - h) and sections 3 and 6 of the L3 report. Until then do not use the lattice moment's rate response for anything quantitative, including the ?lab "lattice flies it" damping.
2. **vx/js/lattice/shaders.js stepFrag wall links on a staircase mask (halfway bounce-back with Ladd's term), for curved walls moving faster than the inflow** (minor). At a surface speed of twice the inflow and more the spinning cylinder loses circulation and its flow turns unsteady where the reference flow is steady; with the Smagorinsky term off the spin-2 case settles on a wrong flow with negative mean lift. The error falls with resolution, so it is the staircase wall and an under-resolved boundary layer, not compressibility. Evidence: Re 200: spin 2 C_L 4.842 (D 12), 4.969 (D 20), 5.419 (D 32) against about 5.6 (Mittal and Kumar 2003 digitised); spin 3 C_L 7.166 at D 20 against 10.366 (tabulated); spin 2 lift swings 0.97 (D 20) at a Strouhal number that changes with the grid; Smagorinsky off: mean C_L -1.005. Half the inflow (surface speed 0.04) leaves spin 2 at C_L 4.766. Spin 0.5 to 1.5 are within 6 percent (section 2). Proposed fix: Interpolated bounce-back with the wall velocity at the true wall point (Bouzidi, Firdaouss and Lallemand 2001), using the signed distance geom.js already computes; then re-run the Magnus series at D 20 and 32. The page stays below a surface speed of 0.05 lattice units (main.js WALL_U_MAX) and is not exposed in practice; the lab's spinning-cylinder sign check is.
3. **vx/index.html Method (the lattice "measures the force on the section as a cross-check", lines near 237 and 263, and the Reynolds limit at 223) and the panel rows CP / LATTICE 2D and CM / LATTICE 2D (vx/js/panel/panel.js lines 89 to 91), with CM_SCALE 1 in vx/js/main.js** (major). The 2D section's normal force, moment and centre of pressure are not a cross-check of Barrowman in any sense the measurements support: the CP sits ahead of the CG at every angle, grid and domain size; the fin increment acts near the CG, not at the fins; and no standard 3D correction brings the section near Barrowman. Evidence: CP 15.3 to 18.2 in from the nose at 2 to 15 deg on 512 x 160 and 1024 x 320 against Barrowman 29.3 to 32.3 in, CG 22 in. Large domain (1024 x 1024): CP 15.6 in at 5 deg. Fin increment at 5 deg acts at 18.7 in (page domain) and carries 1 percent of its force on the fin links. Used as 3D coefficients, total C_N is 21 times Barrowman at 5 deg. Slender-body body plus Diederich-corrected fin increment: CP 15.6 in. Section 5 of the L3 report. Proposed fix: State the lattice as a 2D flow and pressure picture, not a cross-check: either drop the CP / LATTICE 2D and CM / LATTICE 2D rows from the default page (keep them behind ?lab with a 2D-section label), or keep them with the measured limits of section 9 printed beside them. Remove "as a cross-check" from the Method and the glossary.
4. **vx/js/lattice/lattice.js TIERS (grids 3.2 columns per row) with vx/js/geom/geom.js SPAN_FRACTION 0.66 and NOSE_COL_FRACTION 0.12 (the page domain)** (minor). The page domain is 0.47 rocket lengths tall, and at incidence its edges confine the flow: the section's normal force is inflated by about 1.7 times at 5 to 15 deg against a domain 3.2 times taller (the pitch-rate lift only by about 1.1 times). Evidence: C_N at 5 / 10 / 15 deg: 17.3 / 34.8 / 54.5 (512 x 160), 10.0 / 19.1 / 26.7 (512 x 512), 11.0 / 21.3 / 32.9 (1024 x 1024); thin-airfoil theory for a plate as long as the rocket 9.5 / 18.9 / 28.2. The CP does not move with the domain. The engine header (EDGES) already reports the confinement qualitatively. Proposed fix: If the page keeps printing section forces, use a domain at least 1.5 rocket lengths tall (rows = 1.0 x cols with the body at 0.66 cols), which costs 3.2 times the cells, or print only the pressure picture. Not needed for the smoke view.
5. **vx/js/lattice/shaders.js stepFrag collision, the uReg branch (regularised collision, Latt and Chopard 2006), on by default (lattice.js options.regularised, setRegularised)** (minor). At the page's Re 3000 the regularised collision costs lift: on a flat plate it lowers the lift slope by about 11 percent against BGK with Smagorinsky on the same grid (absorbing edges kept in both) and suppresses the lift rise at 6 to 8 deg. The positivity limiter and the speed floor never engage in these runs and change nothing. At Re 1000 the difference is 1.5 percent. Evidence: Plate chord 48, 576 x 288: slope 4.586 (defaults) against 5.134 (BGK + Smagorinsky) per rad at Re 3000; C_L at 6 / 8 deg 0.422 / 0.494 against 0.648 / 0.887; Re 1000: 5.136 against 5.214. Regularised collision alone (limiter off): slope 4.586; positivity limiter alone (BGK collision): 5.134. The defaults' slope at Re 3000 also moves with the grid (chord 32 / 48 / 64: 3.606, 4.586, 4.925), so part of the cost is extra numerical dissipation at under-resolved walls. Proposed fix: Keep the stabilisers where the page needs them (the alpha 15 deg stability gate) but evaluate a less dissipative stable collision at tau0 near 0.502, for example two-relaxation-time (TRT, Ginzburg) or recursive regularisation (Malaspinas 2015), against this plate sweep and the L1 Poiseuille cases before changing the default; quote the plate lift slope with the scheme used.

## 9. Limits the page should state

- Moving-wall term, wall moving along itself: Magnus lift within 6 percent of Mittal and Kumar (2003) at spin 0.5 to 1.5 (Re 200, 20 and 32 cells per diameter) and +7 percent against Kang et al. (1999) at Re 100, spin 1 (Strouhal number -0.6 percent); drag 5 to 14 percent high; at spin 2 lift is 11 percent low at 20 cells and 3 percent low at 32, at spin 3 31 percent low, with a spurious unsteady flow (SIM).
- Moving-wall term, wall moving normal to itself (how setBodyRate pitches the section): the force it produces is 2.0 to 2.5 times the physical one (plate plunge and pitch tests), so the lattice's pitch damping and flick response are not quantitative, not even as 2D numbers (SIM).
- 2D flat plate at Re 1000 (48 cells per chord): lift slope 5.14 per rad, 82 percent of 2 pi and 5 percent under a 2D Navier-Stokes reference; drag within 3 percent of it up to 15 deg; pressure difference within 1 percent of thin-airfoil theory at mid-chord at 4 deg; above 15 deg lift and drag 18 to 26 percent high (SIM).
- At the page's Re 3000 the plate's lift slope depends on the grid, 3.61 (chord 32 cells), 4.59 (chord 48 cells), 4.93 (chord 64 cells) per rad against 2 pi = 6.28; the default regularised collision lowers it by 11 percent against BGK with Smagorinsky (5.13 at chord 48), and the Smagorinsky term itself changes little (4.46 without it). Lifting flow at Re 3000 is under-resolved at the page's cell counts (the section's fins span about 16 cells at tier 1), so section forces carry errors of 10 to 40 percent before any 2D or 3D question (SIM).
- The CHARGER section's centre of pressure is 15 to 18 in from the nose at 2 to 15 deg on both page grids, 11 to 18 in ahead of Barrowman's 29 to 33 in and ahead of the 22 in CG: the 2D section is unstable where the rocket is stable by 4.1 calibres, and this does not change with grid or domain size (SIM).
- In the page's domain the section's C_N at 5 to 15 deg is about 1.7 times its value in a domain 3.2 times taller (confinement); used as a 3D coefficient, as the panel does, it is 16 to 35 times Barrowman's. At the page's pad inflow floor (u_in 0.03, Re 900) it is higher again: C_N 28.1 at 5 deg against 17.3 at u_in 0.10. (SIM)
- In 2D the fins add normal force mostly as extra circulation on the body, acting 18 to 26 in from the nose rather than at the fins (43.6 in); the fin cells themselves carry 0 to 10 percent of it. Neither a finite-wing (Helmbold, Diederich) nor a slender-body correction turns the section into a 3D stability number (SIM, STD).
- Pitch damping from the section has the right sign; its 2D magnitude (-dC_m/d(qD/V) 695 with fins, 1423 without) is unrelated to Barrowman's C_mq (445, 88) and falls when fins are added (SIM).
- Software GL (SwiftShader) ran the engine at 1.9 to 9.4 MCUPS on this shared host (13 to 153 steps per second on 320 x 192 to 512 x 160 grids); the page's tier 3 needs about 470 MCUPS, graphics-card class (SIM).

## 10. What was not done, and why

- Long runs on the engine itself: SwiftShader on the shared host managed 1.9 to 9.4 MCUPS, so the series ran on the CPU transcription with the equivalence of section 1. A graphics card would allow running it on the engine.
- Mittal and Kumar's own table for spin 0.5, 1 and 2: the paper could not be downloaded here (404); the values are digitised from a published plot of their data, checked against their tabulated spin 3 to 5 values (0.8 percent).
- 2D flat-plate data at Re 3000: none retrieved; thin-airfoil theory and the Re 1000 data bracket it.
