# L1: analytic checks of the lattice

Track L1 of VALIDATION.md: analytic checks of the CHARGER field study's lattice engine (vx/js/lattice/lattice.js, shaders.js), run on headless Chromium with SwiftShader, 2026-10-23. Every claim below has a benchmark, a reference with its source, a measured value, the error and a tolerance; one JSON per case is in reports/validation/L1/ and the verdicts are in reports/validation/L1/summary.json. The engine was not changed.

Headline. The scheme the engine documents is implemented exactly (GPU against an independent float32 port: 5e-6 of u_max). In laminar channel flow it is as accurate as halfway bounce-back can be: second order (observed 1.9 to 2.0), the wall error matches the closed-form theory, nu = (tau - 1/2)/3 holds within 0.13 percent at low Mach, the compressibility error scales as Ma^2 (0.15 percent at u_in 0.10), the Smagorinsky term adds exactly (C Delta)^2 |S|, the stabilizers cost nothing in the bulk, and the force reduction counts every link once and conserves momentum. Four things are not as the engine and the page state them. (1) Without the regularized collision or the absorbing layer the scheme is unstable at the page's tau0 even in a creeping channel, because of the outflow edge rule (D1). (2) At u_in 0.10 and the page's Re 3,000, grid-scale noise of about 15 percent of u_in, born where the flow passes the nose shoulder at local Ma 0.3 and more, fills the free stream at tier 1; the Smagorinsky term damps it, and the wall cells run at 1.3 to 1.7 times the stated viscosity (effective Re 1,800 to 2,300 at tier 1, 2,600 at tier 3) (D2). (3) The inflow edge delivers 95 to 97 percent of u_in and about half the cross-flow at incidence, while coefficients are referred to u_in (D3). (4) Float32 storage of the full populations sets an accuracy floor in slow flows (D4). Section 11 lists the limits the page should state.

## Method

Engine under test: vx/js/lattice/lattice.js and shaders.js on branch claude/charming-pascal-wgx7a3, unchanged (no validation hook was needed). Every case drives the public API only: createLattice with a fixed grid (fixedGrid: true, tier 1, autoResetOnNaN: false), setSection, setViscosity({tau0}) or ({re, uRef}), setSmagorinsky, setRegularised, setMachFloor, setStabilisers, setInflow, reset, step, sync, sampleForce, check and lattice.fields.distributions. The state is read back by a copy pass of the three RGBA32F distribution textures into a float render target owned by the bench (vx/js/validation/l1-lattice.js, createReader) and reduced on the CPU in float64.

Runners. Bench page vx/lab/validation/l1.html (window.L1.run(spec), or ?case=... in the address bar), helper vx/js/validation/l1-lattice.js, Node driver scratchpad/validation/L1/run.cjs (Playwright, headless Chromium with --use-angle=swiftshader --enable-unsafe-swiftshader, the repo root served with node's http module; the bench page loads nothing from the network, so no request reaches clarity.ms, bing.com, workers.dev or formspree.io, and nothing is posted). Case plans validation/L1/plan-*.json; generator gen_report.py (verdicts, tables) and write_md.py (this file). Results: one JSON per case in reports/validation/L1/ (name = case), verdicts in reports/validation/L1/summary.json. Independent CPU ports of the scheme in numpy: cpu/lbm_ref.py (float64), cpu/lbm_ref32.py (float32 storage and arithmetic, as the GPU), cpu/bb1d.py (exact force-driven channel), cpu/edge_probe32.py and cpu/periodic32.py (stability probes).

Channel. Fluid rows M to M + H - 1 between solid rows; the engine keeps the outer edge rows fluid, so they sit outside the walls. Halfway bounce-back puts the walls on the cell faces y = M and y = M + H. M = 2 without the absorbing layer, M = 9 with it, so no channel cell lies inside the layer (max(8, 0.05 rows) cells wide). The left edge is the engine's inflow edge (equilibrium at rho 1 and u_in: a flat profile), the right edge its outflow edge (rho 1, zero-gradient velocity and non-equilibrium part). Length 4 H plus inlet and outlet margins (6 H at Re 50 and for the compressibility series), profile station 2 H to 3 H from the inlet (4 H to 5 H in the long channels), pressure gradient fitted over the station plus H/2 on each side. The development check (profile error at x = 1 H to 5 H) is in each JSON (along).

Analytic reference. Plane Poiseuille flow u(y) = 6 Q y (H - y) / H^3 (White 2006, section 3-3.2). The profile is sampled at cell centers, so the analytic profile is scaled to the same discrete sum of the H cell values as the measured one: this is the analytic solution for the measured flow rate, and the comparison is then of shape. Errors: L2 over the H cells relative to the analytic norm; center line: the two cells either side of it; max: the largest cell error over the analytic center-line speed; wall offset: zeros of a least-squares parabola through all H cells, positive when the effective wall lies inside the fluid. Wall theory: for halfway bounce-back the exact discrete steady solution is u = A [y (H - y) + (16 Lambda - 3)/12] with Lambda = (tau - 1/2)^2 for BGK (Ginzburg and d'Humieres 2003; He, Zou, Luo and Dembo 1997) and, shown here, Lambda = (tau - 1/2)/2 for the engine's regularized collision; "L2 beyond the wall theory" is the profile's distance from that discrete solution at the same flow rate, i.e. the part of the error the wall does not explain (compressibility, entrance).

Viscosity. (1) Curvature: in a developed channel dp/dx = rho nu u''; u'' from a parabola fitted to the interior cells (first and last cell left out, so the wall error cannot enter), dp/dx = c_s^2 drho/dx from a linear fit of the channel-mean density along x. (2) Mach-corrected: the compressible channel's convective term d(rho u^2)/dx = -u^2 drho/dx (rho u is constant along x) folded in as (c_s^2 - <u^2>) drho/dx. (3) Pressure drop and flow rate: nu = -(dp/dx) H^3 / (12 rho Q), with the nominal H and with the width between the fitted walls. (4) Locally from the stored non-equilibrium stress: Pi*_xy = -(tau - 1) rho c_s^2 du/dy for the post-collision state (Chapman-Enskog; Krueger et al. 2017, The Lattice Boltzmann Method, ch. 4), giving tau_eff per row.

Steady state. Checks every 500 to 2,048 steps; a run stops when the rms change of the station profile, extrapolated with the slowest viscous mode of the channel (e-folding time H^2 / (pi^2 nu)) or with the observed ratio of successive changes, is below 3e-6 of u_max, or after 13 e-folding times of that mode, or at a step or wall-clock cap (each JSON says which: stopReason). A run that hits a cap is reported as not converged and its numbers are not used for a verdict unless the table says so.

Square. Side s cells, columns 5 s to 6 s - 1 of a (16 s + 1) x 10 s grid (odd widths exercise the halving reduction's bounds), uniform inflow 0.05, Re 20, run until C_D changes by less than 1e-4 over 4 convective units (s / u steps each) after at least 30 units. The reduction is compared with a CPU sum of the per-cell force texture over the same 500-step window (the texture is read right after sampleForce reduces it, with no step in between); the momentum-exchange force is checked against the exact discrete momentum balance of one step: what the streaming step carries into a box around the square minus what leaves it, minus the change of momentum held in the box's fluid cells.

Page conditions. The page's own section (geometry.buildSection at 512 x 160, fins at scale 1, 'through' view, page CG), setViscosity({re: 3000, uRef: 0.10}) as the page sets it, every stabilizer at its default, 4,000 steps (the start-up viscosity has eased back by 2,000). The relaxation time each fluid cell used is recovered from the stored state by inverting the engine's rule tau = max(0.5 (tau0 + sqrt(tau0^2 + 18 C^2 Q / rho)), speed floor, absorbing layer) with Q* = |1 - 1/tau| Q; (tau - tau0)/(tau0 - 1/2) is then (nu_t + nu_floor)/nu0.

## 1. Implementation: GPU engine against independent CPU ports

Before any analytic comparison: does the GPU compute the scheme the engine documents? The same H 16 channel was run in two independent numpy ports of STEP_FRAG and the edge rules (written for this track from the shader source and FIELD.md section 4, not from the GPU code path): one in float64, one with every array in float32 as the GPU stores and computes it. The GPU agrees with the float32 port to 1.8e-6 (tau 0.8) and 4.9e-6 (tau 0.6) of u_max, which is the float32 noise floor of a steady state. Against float64 the GPU differs by 5.6e-6 and 2.8e-5 of u_max; the float32 port differs from float64 by the same 5.7e-6 and 2.4e-5, almost uniformly across the profile (a flow-rate offset of the order of the float32 rounding of rho, amplified by the diffusive time H^2 / nu). So the GPU is the documented scheme, and storing the full populations in float32 (the shaders subtract the rest state w_i only on the float16 path) costs a bias that grows as tau approaches 1/2: 6e-6 at tau 0.8, 2.4e-5 at tau 0.6. That is two orders below the scheme's own error at H 16 and is not a limit the page needs to state, but it is the floor under every number below.

## 2. Halfway bounce-back against the TRT wall theory

The wall error of halfway bounce-back is known in closed form, so it can be separated from everything else. For a force-driven channel the exact steady solution of the discrete scheme is u = F/(2 nu) [y (H - y) + c] with c = (16 Lambda - 3)/12 cells^2, where Lambda is the TRT "magic" product (tau+ - 1/2)(tau- - 1/2) (Ginzburg and d'Humieres 2003, Phys. Rev. E 68, 066614; for BGK, Lambda = (tau - 1/2)^2, which reproduces He, Zou, Luo and Dembo 1997, J. Stat. Phys. 87, 115, and the known exact case tau = 0.933). The engine's regularized collision discards the third-order non-equilibrium moments every step, which is a TRT with tau- = 1, so Lambda = (tau - 1/2)/2. Both statements were checked on an exact 1D (x-invariant) float64 steady state of each collision (cpu/bb1d.py, Guo forcing): the measured c agrees with the formula to 6e-10 cells^2 at twelve points.

## 6. Stabilizers' cost

One laminar channel (H 32, tau 0.6, Re 10, walls 9 rows thick so that the absorbing layer stays outside the channel) with every stabilizer off (plain BGK, the FIELD.md contract without Smagorinsky), then with each one switched on alone, then the engine defaults. Shapes are compared at equal flow rate; the flow rate is listed separately, because the edges decide how much flow enters (section 8).

## 7. Force reduction and momentum exchange

A solid square of side s = 5, 10 and 20 cells in uniform inflow (u_in 0.05, Re 20, blockage 0.1, domain (16 s + 1) x 10 s, odd widths so the halving reduction meets odd sizes at several levels), plain BGK, run until C_D changed by less than 1e-4 over 4 convective units. Three checks: (1) the engine's reduction (2 x 2 halving passes to 1 x 1, read back at 1 x 1) against a float64 CPU sum of the per-cell force texture read back over the same 500-step window, and its link count against the mask and against 12 s - 4; (2) the force of one step against the exact discrete momentum balance of a box around the square (what the streaming step carries in minus what it carries out, minus the change of momentum held in the box's fluid cells), for a box 2 cells and one s around the square; (3) the force against the expected scaling F = C_D (rho u^2 / 2) s at fixed Re and blockage.

## 10. Defects

Model defects the measurements expose (physics unchanged in this phase; each with the evidence and a proposed fix for the fix phase).

**D1. The outflow edge is unstable next to cells relaxing at tau near 1/2 (severity: medium).** Where: vx/js/lattice/shaders.js, STEP_FRAG edge branch (the outflow rule `o = feq(1, u_e) + b - feq(rho_n, u_n)`, which copies the inward neighbor's non-equilibrium part), and lattice.js header EDGES. What: with the absorbing layer off, the contract model (BGK, with or without Smagorinsky 0.12) does not reach a steady state at tau0 0.505 even in a creeping channel (Re 10, Ma 0.002): a disturbance grows from the outflow edge after about 5,000 steps with an e-folding time of about 800 steps and saturates as a chaotic flow at 50 to 70 u_in (runs B-plain-t0.505, B-contract-t0.505, D505-plain+none; positivity limiter and speed floor never act, their runs are bit-identical). Evidence that it is this edge rule: the float32 and float64 CPU ports do the same; an outflow without the copied non-equilibrium part, or with it projected on its second-order Hermite moment, is steady to 24,000 steps; the inflow variant (inflowNeq) does not help; an edge-free periodic channel with the same walls and tau is steady to 60,000 steps (section 6). At tau 0.51 the same channel is steady. The shipped defaults are stable only because the regularized collision or the absorbing layer (tau 0.85 to 0.9 next to the edges) is on; the engine header's gate history (BGK blow-ups at Re 1,500 and 3,000, tau0 0.508 and 0.504) is consistent with this edge mode, which is present at any Re. Proposed fix: regularize the extrapolated non-equilibrium part at outflow (and inflowNeq) cells, n_i = 4.5 w_i Q_iab Pi_ab with Pi from b - feq(rho_n, u_n), as Latt et al. 2008 (Phys. Rev. E 77, 056703) do for straight boundaries; re-run D505-plain+none (expect steady, L2 near 2.4e-3) and the stability gate.

**D2. At u_in 0.10 and the page's Re 3,000, grid-scale noise fills the domain and the Smagorinsky term mostly damps it, so the viscosity that runs is well above the stated nu0 (severity: high for the stated Re 3,000; medium for the flow picture, whose vorticity and pressure views show the noise).** Where: the viscosity setting (lattice.js RE_TARGET 3000 at U_REF 0.10 for every tier, tauFor) together with SMAGORINSKY_C and the rule in STEP_FRAG. What: at tier 1 and u_in 0.10 (u_in / nu0 = 154 per cell) the free stream carries grid-scale velocity noise of 15 percent of u_in (median, p90 43 percent) that does not decay between 4,000 and 8,000 steps; the stress-based strain the Smagorinsky rule sees is 3.7 times the strain of the velocity field; the wall cells run at 1.31 to 1.69 nu0 (alpha 0 to 10; effective Re 2,280 to 1,770) and the median free-stream cell at 1.1 to 1.2 nu0. Tier 3 (u_in / nu0 = 77): wall cells 1.14 nu0 (Re 2,630), free stream 0.39 nu0. With the page's viscosity the noise is absent at u_in 0.03 and 0.05 (local Ma up to 0.18); at u_in 0.10 it is absent at tau0 0.5117 (Re 500), patchy at 0.5058 (Re 1,000) and fills the domain at 0.50195 (Re 3,000): it is born where the flow passes the nose shoulder at local Ma 0.3 and more and spreads as far as the low viscosity lets it. Without Smagorinsky the noise is 1.5 to 3 times larger. Even in a laminar channel at the page's tau and speed (tau 0.505, u_in 0.03, Re 266, H 16) the defaults read 5.7 percent more viscosity than the regularized collision alone gives (P505 runs). Proposed fix (two configurations tested free of noise at tier 1, alpha 5): either lower the top of the inflow range so the local Mach number stays below about 0.2 (u_in 0.05: no noise, wall cells 1.19 nu0, Re = u_in D / nu0 = 1,500 at the page's viscosity), or keep u_in 0.10 and raise tau0 to about 0.51 (Re 500 at tier 1: no noise, wall cells 1.06 nu0; the tier-3 equivalent, Re about 1,000, is not tested); in either case print the effective Re instead of 3,000. A collision that damps the short waves at low viscosity (for example the hybrid recursive regularization of Jacob, Malaspinas and Sagaut 2018, J. Turbulence 19, 1051) with the Smagorinsky strain taken from the resolved velocity field is the fuller fix. Re-run the F cases after any change (expect free-stream noise below 2 percent of u_in and wall-cell viscosity within 20 percent of nu0).

**D3. The inflow edge is not a velocity inlet (severity: low to medium).** Where: STEP_FRAG edge branch, inflow rule `writeEq(uU)` (equilibrium at rho 1 and u_in), and forceFrom in lattice.js (coefficients referred to u_in). What: the mass flux that enters is u_in - (rho_1 - 1)/3 to first order, where rho_1 is the density one cell inside. In the channel the delivered flow was 71, 84 and 91 percent of u_in H at H 16, 32 and 64 (tau 0.8, Re 10; predicted 0.047 against measured 0.045 at H 16), and the absorbing layer lowers it by another 1 percent. On the page section the speed one cell inside the edge is 96.8 and 95.0 percent of u_in at alpha 5 and 10 and only 52 and 61 percent of the cross-flow enters through the windward edge (u_in 0.10). The stated Re and the coefficients (c_n, c_m, C_D, C_L) use u_in and the nominal alpha, so at alpha 5 and 10 they read about 7 and 11 percent low against the dynamic pressure that arrives. Proposed fix: refer Re and the coefficients to the speed and angle measured on a column upstream of the nose (a cheap reduction of one column), or print the delivered speed and angle next to them; the inflowNeq option is not a cure (the header shows it pressurizes the windward plenum further).

**D4. Float32 storage of the full populations limits accuracy in slow, low-viscosity flows (severity: low).** Where: shaders.js LATTICE_COMMON, REST0/REST1/REST8 are zero on the float32 path (the deviation f_i - w_i is stored only on the float16 path). What: against float64 the steady channel is biased by 5.7e-6 of u_max at tau 0.8 and 2.4e-5 at tau 0.6 (H 16), and the H 64 channel at tau 0.6 (u_mean 0.005) stops converging at an L2 error of 2.4e-4 (BGK) and 2.2e-4 (defaults), an irregular pattern above the wall theory's 1.4e-4 and 1.1e-4, so the observed order there is 1.2 and 1.0 instead of 2. A float32 CPU port with DDF-shifting (store g_i = f_i - w_i and sum rho - 1 from them; Lehmann et al. 2022, Phys. Rev. E 106, 015308) cuts the bias from 5.7e-6 to 1.5e-6 (tau 0.8) and from 2.4e-5 to 3.3e-6 (tau 0.6). Proposed fix: compute and store in the shifted form on the float32 path too (the DEV define already does the storage half); re-run L-plain-t0.6-re10-H64 and the implementation check.

Not defects, but worth knowing: the speed floor (MACH_K) is a disclosed stabilizer and does what it says, but at u_in 0.10 it raises the viscosity 8 to 20 times (up to 92 times) in 2 to 13 percent of the fluid cells (section 8); and at tau 0.505 and u_in 0.001 the absorbing layer changes the developed channel profile by 0.3 to 0.6 percent of u_max although the station is outside it (D505 runs), while at u_in 0.03 it does not (P505 runs); the mechanism was not isolated.

## 11. Limits the page should state

Measured, with the conditions; these are the numbers the method section can rely on.

- Straight grid-aligned walls (halfway bounce-back) are second order: observed order 1.89 to 2.00 in exact diffusive scaling for BGK, BGK + Smagorinsky and the engine defaults; profile error 0.05 to 0.08 percent (L2) at 16 cells across, 0.003 to 0.005 percent at 64 (tau 0.8, Re 10). The effective wall sits (3 - 16 Lambda)/(12 H) cells inside the fluid, about 1/(4 H) at the page's tau0. Curved (staircase) walls are not covered here.
- The viscosity is nu = (tau - 1/2)/3 within 0.13 percent at Ma 0.02 (tau 0.55 to 1.0, BGK), within 0.6 percent at tau 0.505 with the regularized collision; the Smagorinsky term adds the predicted (C Delta)^2 |S| in laminar shear (to 0.01 percentage points).
- Compressibility error scales as Ma^2: at u_in 0.10 (Ma 0.17) the profile error is 0.15 percent and a pressure drop over 6 channel heights reads 3 percent high; at u_in 0.03 both are under 0.3 percent.
- At the page's tau0 (0.502 to 0.504) the scheme needs its stabilizers: plain BGK with the contract edges goes chaotic even in a Re 10 channel (outflow-edge mode, D1); the regularized collision or the absorbing layer keeps it steady.
- At u_in 0.10 and the page's Re 3,000 (tau0 0.502 at tier 1, 0.504 at tier 3) grid-scale noise of about 15 percent of u_in fills the free stream at tier 1 (born at the nose shoulder at local Ma 0.3 and more; absent at u_in 0.05 or at tau0 0.51), and the Smagorinsky viscosity it triggers puts the wall cells at 1.3 to 1.7 nu0 at tier 1 (effective Re 1,800 to 2,300) and 1.14 nu0 at tier 3 (Re 2,600); at the pad speed (u_in 0.03) the noise is absent and the wall cells carry 9 percent extra.
- The speed floor raises the viscosity 8 to 20 times in 2 to 13 percent of the fluid cells at u_in 0.10 (tier 1, alpha 0 to 10; 12,600 cells at tier 3, alpha 5); local |u| reaches 0.18 to 0.24.
- The inflow edge delivers 95 to 97 percent of u_in at alpha 5 to 10 and 52 to 61 percent of the cross-flow at u_in 0.10; coefficients are referred to u_in and the nominal alpha, so they read about 7 to 11 percent low against the dynamic pressure that arrives.
- Forces: the reduction counts every wall link once and matches a float64 sum of the per-cell forces to 2e-5; the momentum-exchange force equals the discrete momentum balance to 6e-5; a square's C_D is grid-independent within 0.3 percent from 5 to 20 cells.
- Float32 storage biases a steady flow by 6e-6 to 2.4e-5 of u_max (tau 0.8 to 0.6) and floors a slow H 64 channel (tau 0.6, u_mean 0.005) at 2.2e-4 to 2.4e-4.
- SwiftShader step rates (software GL, not representative of a GPU): 1.4 to 3.6 million cell updates per second unloaded; 13 to 19 steps/s at tier 1 and 4 steps/s at tier 3 under the shared load of this campaign.

## 12. Not done here, and what it would take

- The noise source in D2 is characterized by its dependence on u_in / nu0, Smagorinsky and the speed floor, not traced to a single origin (edges, start-up or the staircase body); confirming the proposed per-tier Re cap or a different collision needs the fix phase (engine change) and a re-run of the F cases.
- The mechanism by which the absorbing layer shifts the tau 0.505 channel profile (0.3 to 0.5 percent) was not isolated.
- Curved walls, moving walls and lift belong to tracks L2 and L3.
